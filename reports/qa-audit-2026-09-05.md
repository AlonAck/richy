# Richy — QA sweep and remediation plan, 5 September 2026

## Context

Customers have complained for two months about four things: screens that get stuck,
dead ends where money goes in and cannot come out, numbers shown in the wrong place,
and screens that look cheap. This sweep looks for those four classes specifically,
plus legal and accessibility exposure, and proposes what to do about it.

**Baseline.** Four audits already exist (`reports/qa-audit-2026-08-17.md`,
`qa-sweep-2026-08-25.md`, `qa-audit-2026-08-30.md`, `qa-audit-2026-09-02.md`).
This sweep does two jobs: **re-verify** whether the last audit's criticals were
actually fixed in the three days since, and **find new ground** — chiefly the
1,629 uncommitted lines of "Business Account v2" that no audit has ever seen.

**Deploy state.** `HEAD` (`440468a`) is pushed and live on Vercel. Everything in
§1, §3 and §4 below is **in front of users right now**. Business v2 (§2) is
uncommitted and is not — those are "fix before you commit", not "fix before Monday".

**Clock.** App Store submission is 23 September (`CAMPAIGN_LAUNCH_2026-10.md:66`).
That is **18 days**.

**Read this before chasing a line number.** `budget-app.jsx` was being edited by
another session *while this audit ran* — it grew from **35,203 to 36,071 lines**
mid-sweep. Line anchors in §1–§4 are pinned to the 35,203-line state and are now
off by roughly +150 to +870 depending on where they sit; anchors in §5 are pinned to

## The short version

**The last audit's Day-1 list was not done.** I re-checked its three most urgent
items against the live tree. All three are still open, including the one it called
a "one-line fix" and put first. `.finally(` is still `0`. Empty `catch` blocks went
from 51 to **53**. `aria-label` coverage went from 47/413 buttons to **46/432** —
it went backwards while 19 new buttons shipped.

**The new work carries two new criticals.** The uncommitted tax-pot ledger can be
driven to a **negative reserve**, at which point the app reports **more spendable
cash than the business actually has**. And "Pay from pot" moves no cash at all.

**But the most serious findings are not bugs at all — they are legal.** Four of them,
and none appears in any prior audit:

- A shipping feature collects users' **phone numbers** and sends financial alerts
  through **Meta**. Neither `privacy.html` nor `terms.html` mentions WhatsApp, Meta or
  phone numbers — and `privacy.html:76` affirmatively lists what Richy doesn't collect.
- **Account deletion does not delete the phone number.** That makes a sentence in the
  live privacy policy false today, and it is exactly what Apple tests under 5.1.1(v).
  It is a one-function fix.
- **Stock Scout asks the model to rank securities with a confidence rating**, on the
  same request where the server guardrail forbids ratings — and its offline fallback
  ranks tickers in plain client code with no guardrail in the loop at all. This was
  recorded as closed on 26 August.
- The App Review notes contain **`[FILL IN before submitting]`** where the demo account
  credentials go, and describe a data-collection footprint that is missing four
  declarable categories.

**Four different formulas compute net worth and they give three different answers.**
The one on the Profile screen **double-counts every savings pot** — so moving $1,000
from your balance into your own savings pot raises the net worth Richy shows you by
$1,000 out of nothing, and can be repeated to farm the "you doubled your net worth"
badges. The one Richard quotes back to you, and the one the Full Analysis score is
built on, **omit your entire investment portfolio**. Details in §5.

**There is a pattern underneath all of it, and it is the thing worth fixing.**
Three separate safety mechanisms in this codebase were fully built, and then never
connected to a button:

| Built | Never wired |
|---|---|
| `RW_ACTIONS` — dismiss / snooze / remind for Richard Watch | `.actions` is written once and never read |
| `deleteTripConfirm` — "Delete this trip? This cannot be undone." | translated into **all four languages**, referenced **nowhere** |
| `buildWatchDigest` / `watchPromptPayload` — the "tell them once, not every morning" system | one occurrence each, dead |

And a second pattern, which is how three "closed" findings turned out to still be
open: **a fix gets scoped to one code path, and the parallel path that produces the
same output survives it.** The 26 August advice guardrail was applied at the API
boundary, so `investPlanOrders`, `investPlanFor` and `localStockScout` — all
deterministic client code — never met it. The tax-pot undo filters on `invoiceId`, so
rows created by the manual control survive it. Both are the same mistake: the fix went
where the *code* was, not where the *output* is.

Nobody is careless here — the code is thoughtful, the comments are genuinely
excellent, and several things I went looking for turned out to be correctly built
(the network layer's timeouts, reduced-motion support, the prompt-injection framing,
the translation dictionaries, the service worker). The problem is that **nothing
catches an unwired feature or a half-scoped fix before it ships**, because:

- **there are no tests** — no test runner, no `test` script, no `*.test.*` file anywhere;
- **there is no CI** — no `.github/`, no lint, no type check;
- **there is no error telemetry** — zero. No Sentry, no crash reporting, no analytics.

So for two months, the only way anyone has learned what users hit is a human reading
35,000 lines by hand, four times. That is why the complaints keep arriving and why
the same defects survive audit after audit.

## 1. Live to users right now

### A. Numbers in the wrong place

**A1 — CRITICAL — the headline number on the home screen reads $0.00 in two taps.**
Still live; reported three days ago as the #1 Day-1 fix.

`safeToSpend` takes the smaller of cash room and budget room:

```js
// budget-app.jsx:9856
var heroBudgetRoom = round2(heroCapRows.reduce(function(s, r) { return s + Math.max(0, r.limit - r.spent); }, 0));
var safeToSpend = Math.max(0, heroCapRows.length ? Math.min(heroCashRoom, heroBudgetRoom) : heroCashRoom);
```

`r.limit` is a **monthly** cap. `r.spent` comes from `spentInCat()`, which is scoped
to the **user-selected timeframe** (`budget-app.jsx:9760`, `inTimeframe(t, timeframe)`).
The timeframe picker sits in the app header (`:35033`) and offers *This Week / This
Month / This Year / All Time* (`:35016`).

Pick **This Year** or **All Time** and every cap compares a year (or a lifetime) of
spending against one month's limit. Every cap reads as blown, `heroBudgetRoom`
collapses to `0`, and the biggest number on the home screen — the one the whole hero
redesign was built around — reads **$0.00**. Pick **This Week** and the same maths
runs the other way: budget room is overstated and the app tells the user they can
safely spend money they cannot.

**A2 — HIGH — a panel headed "This month" shows whatever timeframe is selected, mixed with real month data.**
`budget-app.jsx:10442` renders the literal heading `This month`. The Income and
Spent figures beneath it (`:10455`) are `income`/`expense`, both timeframe-scoped.
"Days left" in the same row is always days left in the **calendar month**. So at
*All Time* the panel is headed "This month" and shows lifetime income next to
days remaining in September.

**A3 — MEDIUM — the "over budget" red state on that panel is unreachable.**
`heroMonthPct` is clamped at 100 (`:9870`, `Math.min(100, ...)`), then the bar asks
`heroMonthPct > 100 ? HNEG : ...` (`:10451`). The condition can never be true. A user
who has spent more than they earned sees amber, never red.

**A4 — MEDIUM — money added to a savings goal above the target is silently destroyed.**
Open since 17 August.

```js
// budget-app.jsx:15314
return g.id === addSheet.id ? Object.assign({}, g, { saved: Math.min(g.target, g.saved + n) }) : g;
```

Goal target $1,000, saved $900, user adds $300 → stored value is $1,000. The extra
$200 is not recorded anywhere, so the "Remove" path (`:15280`) cannot return it —
it can only give back what `saved` holds. No warning, no ledger row, unrecoverable.

Related, and worth a product decision rather than a patch: an unlinked goal's `saved`
figure is not money. It writes no transaction, and `netWorth` is
`balance + savTotal + bizTotal + invTotal` (`:9750`) — goals are not in it. So "add
$500 to a Budget Book" fills a progress ring while the balance, net worth and
`safeToSpend` (A1) all still count that $500 as spendable. There *is* a real-money
path — a goal can be linked to a savings account (`linkedAccountOf`, `~:15130`) — but
the unlinked default is notional, and the button says "Add to Budget Book" with the
result labelled "saved". At minimum the unlinked add sheet needs a line saying the
money hasn't moved.

**A5 — HIGH — a button that says "Cancel Netflix anyway" cancels nothing.**
The goal-rescue screen offers "Cancel both" (`:12663`), "Cancel these 3" (`:12743`)
and `"Cancel " + merchant + " anyway"` (`:12694`). All route to:

```js
// budget-app.jsx:12563
function cancelFindings(ids) {
  var fm = props.foundMoney || { tally: 0, dismissed: [], acted: [] };
  if (props.onSaveFoundMoney) {
    props.onSaveFoundMoney({ tally: fm.tally || 0, dismissed: (fm.dismissed || []).concat(ids), acted: fm.acted || [] });
  }
}
```

It adds the finding to a `dismissed` list. No subscription is touched. The user
believes a recurring charge is gone; next month it arrives. The dismissal is also
permanent — there is no un-dismiss — so the row that would have reminded them is
gone too. This is the most reputationally dangerous item on the page: it is not a
bug users forgive, and it is the kind of copy that draws regulator attention.

### B. Dead ends

**B1 — HIGH — Sign Out throws away unsaved work and cancels the retry that would have saved it.**
Still open; was the last audit's Day-1 item #2.

```js
// budget-app.jsx:34068
function handleLogout() {
  if (saveTimerRef.current) { clearTimeout(saveTimerRef.current); saveTimerRef.current = null; }
  if (saveRetryRef.current) { clearTimeout(saveRetryRef.current); saveRetryRef.current = null; }
  setSaveError("");
  CLOUD.signOut();
  blobRef.current = {};
```

There is no `flushSave()`. The sequence that loses data: user logs a transaction →
network hiccups → the save-failure banner appears → user reacts by signing out →
the queued write is cancelled, the retry is cancelled, and the warning is erased.

The `clearTimeout` is deliberate and correct — the comment explains it prevents a
late write resurrecting a just-deleted account. But `handleLogout` is wired to
**both** the ordinary Sign Out (`:35132`, `onLogout`) **and** account deletion
(`:35139`, `onAccountDeleted`). One function is doing two jobs with opposite needs.

**B2 — HIGH — one tap deletes a savings goal and everything in it, with no confirmation.**

```js
// budget-app.jsx:15266
<button onClick={function() { props.onSaveGoals(props.goals.filter(function(x) { return x.id !== editGoal.id; })); setEditGoal(null); }}
```

The same screen asks the user to confirm **removing $50** from that goal
(`:15273–15288`, a full confirm step with "Yes, do it" / "Not now"). Deleting the
whole goal holding $5,000 asks nothing.

**B3 — HIGH — two rows on the goal-rescue screen permanently rewrite the goal and navigate away.**

```js
// budget-app.jsx:12578
function aimForReal() {
  props.onSaveGoals(props.goals.map(function(g) { return g.id === goal.id ? Object.assign({}, g, { target: aimTargetValue() }) : g; }));
  props.onNavigate("goals");
}
```

`aimForReal` **lowers the user's savings target** to whatever their current rate
projects; `giveOneMoreMonth` (`:12571`) pushes the deadline. Both fire on a single
tap, both leave the screen immediately, and the original number is not stored
anywhere. There is no way back.

**B3b — the manager's literal example is half right, and the half matters.**
"Someone adds money to savings and cannot revert" is **not** true of savings
**accounts**. That system is properly built: `addMoney`/`withdraw` writing to a real
entries ledger (`:22814`), a choice of whether the money returns to the spending
balance or leaves net worth, a custom label and date, a visible move history
(`:23000`), a withdraw button correctly disabled at zero (`:22975`), and closing an
account returns its balance to the main ledger as a transfer (`:22851`).

It **is** true of savings **goals** — the "Budget Books" — which are a different
feature with none of that: no ledger, no history, an add that silently destroys
overflow (A4), and a delete with no confirm (B2). Worth telling the team plainly,
because the complaint reads as "savings is broken" and the fix is in one specific
screen while the other is a model to copy.

**B3c — MEDIUM — deleting an investing trade leaves the position permanently wrong,
and says so.** `deleteActivity` (`:23895`) removes the trade without recalculating,
and the confirm is honest about it: *"Delete this trade? Your position math won't be
recalculated."* (`:24949`). Honest, but the user is left holding an account whose
cost basis is now wrong with no way to recompute it. Credit where due, though — this
area has clearly been worked on: the confirm is real, and deleting a cash entry that
came from the main balance correctly writes a **reversing transaction** back to the
ledger (`:23888–23890`). That is the pattern the goals screen needs.

**B4 — HIGH — "Redo Questionnaire" fires instantly.**
`budget-app.jsx:31921`, `onClick={props.onRetake}` — no confirm. Per the 25 August
audit this flow has no exit once entered and wipes budgets on completion.

**B5 — MEDIUM — trip deletion is instant, and the confirmation for it was written and never used.**
`deleteTripConfirm` — *"Delete this trip? This cannot be undone."* — exists in all
four language dictionaries. Outside those dictionaries the string is referenced
**nowhere in the file**. Someone wrote the safety net, had it translated four times,
and did not connect it.

### C. Stuck screens

**C1 — HIGH — the crash screen's only button re-runs the crash.**

```js
// budget-app.jsx:3849
React.createElement("button", { onClick: function () { location.reload(); } ... }, "Reload Richy")
```

If the crash comes from persisted data — one malformed goal, an undefined field on
a saved budget — reload re-renders the same screen and crashes again. There is no
"go home", no "sign out", no support link. The app is bricked for that account.

The team already knows the right pattern: `BootRetryScreen` (`:34912`) offers both
a retry **and** a sign-out escape. The crash screen just never got it. The screen is
also hardcoded English, outside the theme system, and tells the user *"Your data is
safe in the cloud"* unconditionally — which is not true if the crash followed a
failed save (see B1).

**C2 — MEDIUM — the account-deletion button can stick on "Deleting…" forever.**
`budget-app.jsx:31353–31378`. The handler itself is well built — a `delBusy` guard,
error state cleared on every branch, partial-failure handling, a support address.
But the `fetch` at `:31360` has no timeout or `AbortController`. If the request
hangs, `delBusy` never clears. This is the flow Apple requires under 5.1.1(v), so it
is the worst one to have hang.

**C3 — HIGH — Escape and Android Back close 3 of the app's 11 modals, and on the
other 8 the Back gesture navigates the screen *underneath* the modal that is still
covering it.**

```js
// budget-app.jsx:34851 — closeTopLayer, the whole list of what it knows about
if (document.querySelector("[data-richard-history], [data-richard-chat-panel]")) { … return true; }
if (sheet) { setSheet(false); return true; }
if (timeframeMenuOpen) { setTimeframeMenuOpen(false); return true; }
return false;
```

There are **11** full-screen `position:fixed; inset:0` overlays. For the other eight —
the trip wizard (`:16116`, `:16134`), the business wizard (`:28039`, `:28206`), the
stock trailer (`:26599`), Bank Sync (`:30876`), the confirm sheet (`:31027`) —
`closeTopLayer` returns `false`, so Escape does nothing and `backRef.current()`
(`:34864`) falls through to `navStackRef.current.pop()`. The user presses Back to get
out of a modal, and instead the app changes the page behind it while the modal stays
on screen. On Android that is the system Back button. This is a stuck screen in the
most literal sense the complaints describe.

Related: `aria-modal` appears **once** in the file, `role="dialog"` twice, `inert`
never, and no overlay traps focus — so Tab walks out of the modal into the page
behind it.

**C4 — the "0 `.finally()`" figure is real but the framing needs correcting.**
`.finally(` is still `0` and empty `catch` blocks are now **53** (was 51). But I read
them: the large majority are correct defensive guards around browser APIs —
`localStorage`, WebGL teardown, `setPointerCapture`. Those are fine and should not be
counted as debt.

The network layer is genuinely well engineered and deserves saying so: `fetchStock`
(`:1720–1730`) has a 15-second timeout, an inflight-map that always clears, and a
`settled` latch so exactly one outcome reaches waiters — with a comment explaining
the stuck-forever bug it was written to fix. `callClaude` does the same with a 55s
abort. **The stuck-screen risk is not spread across the app; it is concentrated in
the Firestore write path and in the handful of fetches that bypass those helpers**
(`:1679`, `:1905`, `:6761`, `:30862`, `:31193`, `:31360`, `:34572`). That is a much
smaller and more tractable job than "53 empty catches" suggests.

### D. Screens that look cheap

**D1 — CRITICAL — all four Richard Watch screens render a raw code identifier as their title.**
Reported three days ago as a one-line fix. **Not done.** I verified it end to end:

- the header falls through to `tr(currentTab)` (`budget-app.jsx:35040`, final branch);
- `tr()` returns the key itself when it misses (`:1439`, `|| key`);
- `watchBrief:`, `watchGoal:`, `watchForecast:` and `watchOuts:` appear as dictionary
  keys **zero times** — I grepped each one.

So the sticky header on the Daily Brief reads the literal string **`watchBrief`** —
in English, Hebrew, Arabic and Russian alike. It is what a user sees the moment they
tap Richard's card on the home screen, and it is what an App Store reviewer will see.

**D2 — HIGH — the app's system chrome is purple; the app is cream.**
`index.html:8` — `<meta name="theme-color" content="#8970C6"/>`. The runtime only

also `#141210`, so the PWA splash flashes near-black before a cream app loads. Three
different brand colours across three files. There are also **66** hardcoded instances
of the legacy purple (`rgba(137,112,198,…)` ×29, `#8970C6` ×37) scattered through the
app — e.g. a purple border on a brand-orange button at `budget-app.jsx:31922`.

**D3 — MEDIUM — three AI chat screens use a typed caret as the send icon.**

**D4 — HIGH — 21 screen titles are hardcoded English.**

**D5 — MEDIUM — the privacy eye leaves the actual balance on screen.**
On the Safe-to-spend panel the blur wraps only the derived figure

## 2. Not yet live — uncommitted "Business Account v2"

1,629 changed lines, never reviewed. Two criticals; **do not commit as-is.**

**2.1 — CRITICAL — the tax pot can go negative, and then the app reports more cash than exists.**

Reserve rows created by an invoice carry an `invoiceId`; rows created by the manual
reserve/release control do **not**:

```js
// budget-app.jsx:27635 — moveTaxPot, no invoiceId on the row
entries: ents.concat([{ id: Date.now(), kind: mode === "release" ? "release" : "reserve", amount: v, date: today, label: label || (mode === "release" ? "Tax paid" : "Set aside") }])
```

Un-paying an invoice filters the pot by that key:

```js
// budget-app.jsx:27603
patch.taxPot = Object.assign({}, biz.taxPot || {}, { entries: potEnts.filter(function(e) { return e.invoiceId !== invId; }) });
```

A release row has `invoiceId === undefined`, so `undefined !== invId` is true and the
row **survives** while its matching reserve is deleted. `bizTaxReserved` has no floor
(`:21627–21632`), so it returns a negative number, and:

```js
// budget-app.jsx:21633
function bizSpendableCash(biz) { return round2(businessCash(biz) - bizTaxReserved(biz)); }
```

subtracting a negative **adds**. Worked example: $5,000 invoice paid at 25% reserves
$1,250 → owner pays the tax bill, releases $1,250 → reserved $0 → the cheque bounces
and the owner un-marks the invoice → reserved is now **−$1,250**, and the tax screen
tells them they have **$1,250 more spendable cash than they do**. `deleteInvoice`
(`:27649`) has the same path.

**2.2 — CRITICAL — "Pay from pot" moves no money.**
`moveTaxPot` (`:27628–27638`) writes only to `taxPot.entries`; the cash ledger is
untouched, so `businessCash()` and net worth do not move. Treating a *reserve* as a
pure earmark is right and the design comment (`:21622–21626`) argues it well — but
*release* is being used for two different things: "I no longer need to earmark this"
(no cash movement, correct) and "I have paid the tax bill" (cash must leave). The
button says **"Pay from pot"** and the copy says *"Releasing money from the pot when
you pay the bill"* (`:29768`). The owner pays the tax authority and the app still
shows the money.

**Other findings in this diff** (severity, anchor):

- HIGH — "Graduate" wipes the whole roadmap (`roadmap: null`, `:27334`) from a bare
  button (`:29566`), no confirm, no undo.
- HIGH — "Delete invoice" is one tap, no confirm (`:28720`), and silently removes the
  revenue row and tax reserve it created. Capital-history delete two screens away
  *does* confirm (`:29332–29343`) — so it is inconsistent as well as unsafe.
- HIGH — the tax pot is invisible to Richard: `bizContextLine` still reports full
  `businessCash` (`:22027`), never `bizSpendableCash`, while the new chat chip asks
  *"Can I still pay myself with tax due?"* (`:28539`).
- HIGH — "Q*n* set-aside" compares an **all-time** reserve total (`:21628`, no date
  filter) against a **single-quarter** liability (`:28767`). Q1 carry-over makes Q2
  read "Covered" when nothing was set aside for Q2.
- HIGH — unsigned `dollars()` on values that can go negative: "Spendable cash"
  (`:28753`) and "Profit · 3 mo avg" (`:28862`). `dollars()` is unsigned by design
  (`dollarsSigned()` exists and is used correctly 270 lines away at `:29024`), so a
  −$400 overdraft prints as "$400.00" with only colour to distinguish it.
- HIGH — reserving is uncapped against available cash; releasing is capped
  (`:29779`). A business holding $300 can earmark $10,000.
- HIGH — **zero `tr()` calls in 1,199 added lines.** ~110 English literals. The tab
  labels bypass translation structurally: `GlassTabBar` only calls `tr()` for tabs
  without an icon (`:33113`, `{t.icon ? t.label : tr(t.id)}`) and every business tab
  has one (`:21719`).
- MEDIUM — coverage percentage can print negative ("−45% covered", `:28731`/`:28775`)
  once 2.1 fires; `max={taxDue.owed || 1}` (`:28770`) fills the bar when $0 is owed.
- MEDIUM — the tab-bar summary contradicts the screen for idea-stage accounts: the
  bar shows profit (`:21741`), the hero shows spend (`:29002`) — "+$300" vs "$200".
- MEDIUM — a paid invoice's breakdown is recomputed from the *current* rate/toggle
  (`:28646`), not what was written at payment time, so it can assert a split that
  never happened.
- MEDIUM — "Mark unpaid" (`:28719`) and per-expense delete (`:29287`) both destroy
  ledger rows with no confirm.
- LOW — `id: Date.now()` in three writers (`:27583`, `:27606`, `:27635`) collides
  within a millisecond; `toggleInvoicePaid` already works around it with `+ 1`.

**Verified clean in this diff, worth recording:** the tax-pot *reversal path is
reachable* (this was my main suspicion and it does not hold — release mode, a
reachable "Pay from pot" button, a dismissible overlay); invoice paid/unpaid is
symmetric for the normal case; business close **and** delete both confirm and name
what is lost; all six loading flags clear as the first statement of their callback,
so **there are no stuck screens in this diff**; no hardcoded `$`; division guards
present everywhere they matter.

## 3. Legal and compliance — the most serious section in this report

## 5. Second sweep — debt, IOUs, trips, household, badges and CSV import

A second pass covered the areas §1 could not reach. Anchors here are pinned to the
**36,071-line** tree. I re-verified the two criticals myself.

### The net worth problem — four formulas, three answers

**5.1 — CRITICAL — the Profile screen double-counts every savings pot, and the
net-worth badges can be farmed by moving your own money around.**

```js
// budget-app.jsx:3291 — motivSnapshot
var allIncome  = tx.filter(function(t) { return t.type === "income"  && !isTransfer(t) && motivSettled(t, todayIso); })...
var allExpense = tx.filter(function(t) { return t.type === "expense" && !isTransfer(t) && motivSettled(t, todayIso); })...
var netWorth = round2(allIncome - allExpense + savTotal + businessTotal(...) + investingTotal(...));
```

Overview computes `balance + savTotal + bizTotal + invTotal` (`:10038`) and
deliberately does **not** filter transfers, because the −$1,000 transfer out of the
balance is what `+ savTotal` cancels. This formula filters transfers out **and still
adds `savTotal`**, so the cancellation never happens: move $1,000 into a savings pot
and Profile says you are $1,000 richer. Repeat as desired.

It sits directly beneath a comment promising *"so Profile can never disagree with
Overview about what the user is worth"* (`:3287`). It feeds the `h-01`…`h-06` badges
(`netWorth >= opening × 2/3/5/10/100`, `:3237`) and `cushionMonths`. In a money app,
a badge congratulating someone for tripling a net worth they did not triple is worse
than a wrong number — it is the app lying in a celebratory voice.

**5.2 — HIGH — the net worth Richard quotes, and the one Full Analysis scores you on,
leave out your entire investment portfolio.** I checked all three call sites:

| Site | Includes `investingTotal`? |
|---|---|
| Goals `netWorthCalc` (`:15464`) | **yes** |
| Advisor (`:18266`) | **no** |
| Full Analysis (`:21105`) | **no** |

A user with $50k invested sees one figure on the dashboard and hears a figure $50k
lower from Richard in the next breath. `bufferMonths` and the "months of breathing
room" copy (`:21110`) are computed from the understated number.

**5.3 — HIGH — those same two formulas are bare `tx.reduce` calls with no `isSettled`
guard**, so they count pending and future-dated rows that the dashboard has not
booked. A pending rent row drags Richard's net worth down while Overview still shows
the money.

**5.4 — MEDIUM-HIGH — Overview's budget rows count pending spend; the "Spent"
headline six lines above them does not.** `spentInCat` (`:10047`) has no `isSettled`,
while the headline (`:10044`) does. So a budget can render red purely from a row the
user ticked "Mark as pending", and the per-category numbers do not sum to the total
printed beside them.

**5.5 — MEDIUM — Overview's "today" is UTC and Profile's is local**, so the two
screens disagree about which transactions have happened. `:10025` uses
`toISOString()`; `:3290` uses `isoDay()` (`getFullYear/getMonth/getDate`). The new
transaction form defaults to the UTC date (`:13088`). This is the long-running
UTC/local split, still live, now with a comment at `:2790` claiming the two use the
"same rule" when they do not.

### More dead ends

**5.6 — HIGH — a CSV import commits hundreds of rows with no batch record and no
undo.** `onImport={function(txs) { props.onSaveTx(props.tx.concat(txs)); }}`

bank-sync path, which stamps `synced: true` (`:11324`). Pick the wrong amount column,

**5.7 — HIGH — and the import preview never shows the date**, which is the field most

**5.8 — HIGH — "Leave household" is one unguarded tap, and it clears local state

**5.10 — MEDIUM — the debt payoff tracker has no way to record a payment.** `DebtView`

## 6. What I think the right move is

### 6.1 The one thing to do first is not a bug fix

The last audit recommended a pre-commit gate and it was right, but it was proposed as
an afternoon of lint rules. I would go one step further, because the evidence now
says the problem is **absence of signal**, not absence of discipline:

**Install error telemetry before fixing anything else. It is under a day.**

Zero crash reporting exists. Had a reporter been live on 30 August, the `watchBrief`
header would have been a ticket within an hour of the first tap — instead it has now
survived two audits and is still on screen. Every finding in this report was found by
a human reading 35,000 lines, which is why they arrive weekly instead of hourly, and
why the manager hears about them from customers first.

**Then the gate,** four checks that fail the build:

1. every value `currentTab` can take must resolve to a real dictionary key —
   *this alone would have caught D1 before it shipped*;
2. `T.ink3` must equal the passing hex, so the contrast fix cannot drift back;
3. no new `toISOString()` date site;
4. any new busy flag must have a matching `.finally(`;
5. no literal ticker symbol or `confidence` field may appear in a render path — the
   check that would have caught 3.3, 3.4 *and* the still-open `investPlanOrders`
   finding in one rule. **Put the advice guardrail where financial strings are
   rendered, not at the API boundary** — that is the concrete lesson from three
   separate escapes.

### 6.2 Then, in this order, against 18 days

**Day 1 — the legal criticals, because they are cheap and they are false statements.**
3.2 (add the two WhatsApp collections to `api/delete-account.js` — one function; a
live privacy-policy sentence is untrue until it lands). 3.1 (add Meta/WhatsApp and the
phone number to `privacy.html`, and correct the "does not collect" line). 3.3 + 3.4

### 6.3 Why the 18 days matter more than they look

The service worker is well built — a build-id-stamped cache, network-first
navigations, dev caching skipped (`sw.js:20–26`). Web and PWA users pick up a fix on
their next navigation, so nothing above is stuck behind a stale cache today.

But `sw.js:8–12` records that **iOS native does not run it** — Capacitor bundles the
runtime into the app. So the moment Richy is submitted, the cost of fixing any item
in this report changes from *a deploy* to *an App Store review cycle*. Everything in
§1 is cheap to fix this week and expensive to fix on 24 September.

### 6.4 The one thing I would push back on

**Keep fixes and features in separate commits.** `88fc56d` bundled four TIER 0 fixes
with a 400-line hero rewrite, and A1 — a live critical on the app's headline number —
rode in on the back of fixes that were individually reviewable. The fixes would have
shipped just as fast alone. Business v2 is the same shape of risk sitting in the
working tree right now: 1,629 lines, one commit, two criticals.
