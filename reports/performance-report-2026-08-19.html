Executive Summary

Across 12 runs since Jun 19, first-session performance is volatile, not improving on a stable trend — kept-rate swings from 0% to 60% run to run with no clean upward line, and every single metric in your own dashboard target (first impression ≥6, buy-intent ≥50%) has been missed more often than hit. The one clean win in the whole dataset is a controlled same-day test on Jul 27: surfacing already-built features (bank sync, debt payoff, add-partner) as quick-action cards on the home screen lifted kept-rate from 40% to 60% and buy-intent from 19.4% to 29% in a single change — direct proof that discoverability, not new capability, is your highest-leverage lever right now.

The headline concern: that same win is built on the one thing your Aug 17 QA audit already flagged as actively harmful — the surfaced “Sync your bank” card is a demo-only Israeli-bank simulation that writes fabricated transactions into the real ledger. Bank sync / auto-import is the single most-mentioned gap in all 12 runs (91 total mentions, present in every run without exception), and the metric lift you just measured came from making that unmet promise more visible, not from closing it. That's borrowed trust, and it's due.

What Worked
  • Surfacing existing features beats building new ones. The Jul 27 fix didn't ship a single new capability — it moved bank sync, debt payoff, and add-partner from buried menus to home-screen quick-action cards. Result: kept-rate 40%→60%, buy-intent 19.4%→29%, NPS +4.5→+5.5, in one same-day test.
    “The "Add your partner — share budgets & goals" card right on the home screen. My whole hesitation was the single-income setup, and here was the answer sitting in front of me: put Sivan on it and we see one shared picture.”Nadav Peretz · working parent · flipped from would-give-up to kept
  • Problem-specific onboarding copy converts fast. Personas whose exact situation gets named early (freelancer, debt-focused, newly-married) consistently post higher NPS and WTP than generic ones, even when the underlying friction (manual entry) is identical.
    “That ₦ is a real, properly formatted currency and Richard actually understood my allowance problem instead of giving generic American advice”Tunde Adebayo · college student, Nigeria
  • Richard (the AI advisor) is a genuine differentiator when it gets concrete. The strongest praise across all 12 runs is consistently about Richard doing real math against a persona's actual numbers, not generic advice — this is the moat your roadmap already bets on.
    “Watching it turn my guess into $72,000 a year and $360,000 over five years, and then telling me roughly $3,600 a month of that is recoverable. That's the first time anyone has…”Maya Rosenfeld · high earner / low saver
  • Localization that exists gets rewarded disproportionately. Naira, shekel/euro dual display, and Russian-language mode each turned a would-be churner into a kept user on their own, even with other friction (manual entry) unresolved.
What Needs Improvement
Bank sync / auto-import appears in all 12 of 12 runs — 91 total mentions, more than 2.5× the next most-requested theme. It is simultaneously your #1 growth lever and, per the Aug 17 QA audit, a live trust and regulatory liability: the “Sync your bank” card promises a real integration but is a Leumi-only demo that writes fabricated transactions into the real ledger with no bulk-remove. Two of four simulated quitters in the Jul 27 post-fix run named this card by name as their reason for leaving — the same card that drove the metric win.
  • Discovery raised the promise faster than the product can keep it.
    “Seeing "Sync your bank" up front lifted her hopes, but the sync is for an Israeli bank that isn’t hers, and the CatchUp recall wall plus manual entry still stood between her and an answer.”Yael Ben-Ami · college student, Israel · Jul 27 PM
  • Kept-rate has no stable floor. 0%–60% run to run with no fix in between (Jun 19–26) suggests first-session experience is still fragile and highly sensitive to which 10 personas happen to land, not yet a robust default experience.
  • Buy-intent lags engagement everywhere. Peak buy-intent-at-$5 across all 12 runs is 29% — still under your own 50% pass bar in every single run. People are willing to try Richy; monetization readiness isn't there yet.
  • Irregular/dual income and localization gaps are structural, not edge cases. Both recur in all 12 runs. Onboarding still defaults to a single fixed “monthly income” field, which misreads freelancers and dual-income couples — a meaningful slice of “young people trying to build wealth,” not a niche.
  • App Store copy is ahead of reality. Per the Aug 17 QA audit, the listing claims full RTL support while signup/onboarding/CatchUp have zero translation coverage — a Hebrew user's first ten minutes are English text in an RTL shell. This is a submission-risk item, not just a UX gap.

Insights and Observations
  • Your best-measured win is standing on your worst-flagged risk. The Jul 27 A/B result and the Aug 17 QA audit are about the same card, pointing opposite directions. Read together, not separately: the fix that improved sim metrics most is the fix your own code audit says is currently doing net harm in production. Ship the trust fix before you lean further into the discoverability play, or you'll be optimizing a metric that's actively costing you App Store reviews.
  • Sim data can't see the bugs that matter most for retention. Every sim run is a single first session. The Aug 17 audit's silent data-loss bugs (debt-edit kills saving, edit corrupts opening balance, boot-splash hangs) wouldn't show up in a 10–15 minute first-session test — they show up on someone's third or fourth open, which is exactly the session a real App Store reviewer or early adopter will have. Treat “good sim score” and “safe to grow on” as two different questions.
  • With n=10 personas, single-run swings under ~15–20pp are noise. Don't over-read week-to-week wiggles in kept-rate; the Jul 27 pair is trustworthy specifically because it held cohort methodology constant and changed one variable.
